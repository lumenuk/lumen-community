# Lumen Growth Product & Package Strategy

## 1. Purpose

This skill defines what Lumen Growth sells, to whom, and how offers must be structured and confirmed before publishing. It exists to prevent premature or incorrect pricing/package content from shipping to the live site.

## 2. When to use this skill

- Before building the Services page or any pricing/package component.
- Before writing copy that mentions specific package names, prices, inclusions, or contract lengths.
- Before designing a pricing table, comparison grid, or "recommended package" UI.
- Any time the user asks to "finalise" or "publish" services/pricing.

## 3. Core rules

**Target market:**
- B2B, service-based, London-based. Buyer is a business owner or senior decision-maker, not a consumer.
- Audience: London business owners broadly. Do not name target industries on the site (owner rule, July 2026).
- Primary target areas: London broadly, plus specifically Battersea, Greenwich, Hackney, Tower Hamlets, Fulham, Richmond.
- Restaurants and other low-margin businesses are explicitly not a core target. They may be mentioned only as suited to a lighter, social-media-only support offering — never featured as a flagship example.

**REPOSITIONED OFFERING (July 2026) — community first:**
- Core: Lumen Community membership. Concrete inclusions (owner-confirmed 2026-07-13): one-to-one check-ins monthly or quarterly plus ad-hoc calls when something urgent comes up; a members' group chat (WhatsApp); LinkedIn networking; in-person London meetups and events; partner perks. Applications via /contact.
- MEMBERSHIP TERMS (owner-confirmed 2026-07-13): first week free; the site may say founder members join with "no tie-in". Internal ladder (context only, do not publish): free week → month with no tie-in → 6-month tie-in for later members. Membership fee figures stay unpublished; fee is discussed on the application call.
- FOUNDER MEMBERS: the first clients are founder members and get lower monthly rates than after the community grows past roughly 10–20 members/clients. The site may say "founder members join at lower monthly rates" and "we take on a limited number of businesses at a time" — never publish the 10–20 figure or any rates.
- Services arm leads with SOCIAL MEDIA: content creation, account management, strategy, growth, across Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn. Open to non-members, but positioned as members-first / limited capacity.
- General marketing and SEO are positioned as guidance and support (advice, basic on-page SEO, Google Business Profile support), NOT full-service delivery. SEO audits are outreach-only; the site offer is the free Content Audit.
- No sector/industry targeting language anywhere.

**Legacy service categories (context only, do not build agency pages around these):**
- SEO
- Local SEO
- Google Business Profile optimisation
- Social media strategy
- Social media management
- Paid advertising
- Email marketing
- LinkedIn marketing
- Website improvements
- Landing pages
- Blog/content writing
- Review/reputation strategy
- Analytics/reporting
- PR/partnership-style visibility where relevant

**NO PUBLIC PRICES — owner-confirmed policy (July 2026):**
The site never displays prices, contract lengths, or package inclusions as commitments. An internal pricing sheet exists (supplied by the owner on 2026-07-07 — "Website & Local SEO Packages"; kept off-site) but it is context for sales calls, NOT approved website copy. Final package names, inclusions, contract length, and prices remain unconfirmed for publication.

On-site package presentation (the approved pattern):
1. Three package cards, structured as indicative scope tiers (lighter / core / fuller), with **no prices anywhere** — no "from £X", no ranges, no monthly figures.
2. The middle card reads as the recommended starting option — visually emphasised, without invented numbers or fake "most popular" social proof.
3. Copy must state that packages are **"shaped after the Growth Audit"** — scope, pricing, and contract length are confirmed after the audit and a call.
4. Package names are owner-confirmed (July 2026): **Visibility**, **Core Growth** (recommended), **Full Presence**. Don't rename them without a new owner instruction. Inclusions shown on cards remain indicative scope, not commitments.

Do not add prices, discounts, or contract terms to the site without a new, explicit owner instruction that supersedes this policy.

## 4. Business-specific instructions

- Structure the Services page services-first (categories with concrete outcomes), with the three-tier package section following the pattern above — tiers describe scope shape, never priced menus.
- When describing services, tie each one to a concrete outcome for the target industries (e.g. Local SEO → more enquiry calls for a dental clinic in Battersea; Google Business Profile optimisation → more direction requests and calls for a gym; review/reputation strategy → more trust for a beauty clinic where trust concerns are high).
- Revenue/profit band question on the lead form should be phrased carefully and framed as optional/approximate — this belongs to `lumen-conversion-system`, but package recommendations (if ever automated) must not directly gate access; they only help Lumen Growth prepare for the call.
- No self-serve checkout or "buy now" anywhere. Every commercial path ends at a Growth Audit request, never a payment page. Pricing, contracts, and payment (mostly bank transfer) happen off-site after a call — do not build any on-site payment flow.
- Anywhere packages are mentioned, caveat in copy that scope and pricing are confirmed after a Growth Audit call based on the business's specific needs.

## 5. Things to avoid

- Never invent final package names, prices, or inclusions without user confirmation, even as a "temporary" placeholder that looks finished — placeholders must look like placeholders.
- Never build a checkout, cart, or payment flow.
- Never gate content behind a paywall.
- Never feature restaurants or low-margin businesses as primary target-industry examples.
- Never promise guaranteed outcomes (rankings, revenue, lead volume) tied to a package.
- Do not add contract/legal specifics (cancellation terms, refund policy) beyond what the user has confirmed — these are commercial decisions, not content-writing decisions.

## 6. Quality checklist

Before marking any services/pricing work as done:
- [ ] Is the site completely free of prices, "from £X" figures, and contract-length commitments (per the no-public-prices policy)?
- [ ] Do package cards follow the approved pattern: three tiers, no prices, middle tier recommended, "shaped after the Growth Audit" caveat present?
- [ ] Does every service tie back to a real outcome for one of the named target industries?
- [ ] Is there zero on-site payment or checkout functionality?
- [ ] Are restaurants/low-margin businesses absent from flagship examples, and only mentioned (if at all) as suited to lighter social-only support?
- [ ] Does the page avoid guaranteed-outcome language?

# Lumen Growth Copywriting System

## 1. Purpose

`lumen-brand-system` defines *who Lumen Growth is*. This skill defines *how to actually write the sentences* — headline structure, rhythm, CTA phrasing, and per-page copy patterns — so copy is specific and commercial rather than vague or templated.

## 2. When to use this skill

- Whenever drafting real page copy (headlines, body copy, CTAs, form labels, microcopy).
- When writing service descriptions, FAQ answers, or blog intros.
- When reviewing drafted copy for specificity and banned-phrase compliance.

## 3. Core rules

**Headlines:**
- Lead with the commercial outcome or the specific problem, not an abstract aspiration. Formula: [specific outcome/problem] + [for whom/how], e.g. "Marketing for London businesses that already do good work — they just aren't easy to find." Not: "Unlock your business's true potential."
- Keep primary headlines under ~14 words. If it needs a subhead to make sense, the subhead should add a concrete detail (who it's for, what happens next), not repeat the headline in other words.
- Every headline must pass this test: could this sentence appear verbatim on a generic SaaS template? If yes, rewrite with something specific to London/local B2B marketing.

**Sentence rhythm:**
- Mix short and medium sentences. Avoid long compound marketing sentences stacked with adjectives.
- One idea per sentence. Cut adjectives that don't add information ("innovative," "cutting-edge," "world-class").
- Write like you're explaining it to a smart business owner over a coffee, not pitching a boardroom of marketers.

**CTA phrasing:**
- Primary CTA is always some form of "Request a Growth Audit" / "Get a Growth Audit" / "Start Your Growth Audit" — keep exact wording consistent per `lumen-brand-system`'s naming rule.
- Secondary CTAs (e.g. "See our services," "How it works") should be plain and literal, not clever.
- Never use urgency-manufacturing CTA copy ("Limited spots," "Act now," "Don't miss out") — this reads as low-trust for a serious B2B service and contradicts the credible/corporate positioning.

**Site pages (owner-confirmed list, July 2026 repositioning):** Home · Community · Services · Blog · Contact · FAQ · Privacy Policy · Terms · 404. (Agency-era Growth Audit and How It Works pages removed; redirects in next.config.ts.)

**Per-page copy patterns:**
- **Home (owner-confirmed 4-section structure, July 2026 repositioning):** 1. Hero — Lumen Growth as a London business community; primary CTA "Apply to Join the Community", secondary "Request a Free Growth Audit". 2. What We Do — 3-5 sentences of prose (community, social media support, marketing guidance, network), one approved soft line about bigger moments, no CTAs, no industries. 3. The Community — gold/warm-accent-dominant section describing the Lumen Community; one button "See the Community" → /community. 4. Services — social media first, brief marketing/SEO support mention, map animation, buttons "View Our Services" + "Join the Community". No founder narrative as the hook.
- **What We Do / How It Works:** Walk through the real process (see `lumen-conversion-system` for the 8-step journey) in plain numbered/staged language. Explain what happens after someone enquires — this reduces anxiety about "will I get spammed/upsold."
- **Services:** Each service gets a short, outcome-oriented description tied to a named industry example (see `lumen-product-strategy`). Avoid listing features with no outcome attached.
- **Growth Audit / Contact:** Copy should explain what a Growth Audit actually is (a review of current visibility/trust/marketing, followed by a call), set expectations (no obligation, no hard sell), and reduce friction/anxiety about handing over business details.
- **FAQ:** Answer real objections a skeptical business owner would have (cost, contract length, results timeline, who does the work, data privacy) — not generic filler questions.
- **Blog/Insights:** Practical, specific, London-local angle where possible (e.g. "What Google Business Profile changes mean for London clinics"). Avoid generic "10 marketing tips" listicle voice.
- **Privacy Policy / Terms:** Plain-English legal copy, GDPR-aligned (pair with `lumen-security`) — clarity over legal flourish, but still legally coherent; flag to the user that final legal copy should get a lawyer's review before this is treated as legally sufficient.

## 4. Business-specific instructions

- Every page should be able to answer "why should I trust this specific agency with my specific business" — so copy should reference real specifics (named industries, named London areas, named services, a real explained process) rather than staying abstract enough to apply to any agency anywhere.
- Because the business is new, copy must never imply an established track record it doesn't have ("trusted by hundreds of businesses," "years of proven results"). Instead lean on specificity of process, clarity of offer, and directness about who this is for.
- When writing about outcomes, use plausible, industry-grounded language (e.g. "more enquiry calls," "better visibility in local search," "more completed bookings") rather than fabricated numbers or percentages.

## 5. Things to avoid

- Never use: "unlock your potential," "supercharge growth," "AI-powered solutions," "we scale brands," "take your business to the next level," "game-changing," "revolutionize," "seamless," "holistic," "synergy."
- Never write a headline that could be copy-pasted onto an unrelated SaaS product without edits.
- Never manufacture urgency or scarcity.
- Never state or imply unverified results, client counts, or years of experience.
- Never let a CTA vary in wording across pages for the same action (keep "Growth Audit" consistent).

## 6. Quality checklist

- [ ] Does every headline pass the "could this be on a generic SaaS template" test?
- [ ] Is the primary CTA wording identical everywhere it appears?
- [ ] Does each service description name a concrete outcome and/or a named target industry?
- [ ] Is there zero fabricated social proof, stat, or track-record claim?
- [ ] Does the copy read like a specific London B2B agency, not a template with the word "Lumen" swapped in?
- [ ] Are FAQ answers addressing real objections, not filler questions?
